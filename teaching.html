In my teaching, I implement a student-centered approach which leverages active learning. I value in-class time as an opportunity not only to lecture but also to facilitate shared learning among students and direct communication between students and myself. I design in-class activities for multiple settings: small group discussions, individual written work, students orally presenting their solutions to the class, and students engaging with poll-based assessments.


Success in teaching to me is when students from disparate backgrounds and levels of computational experience indicate that my course has enabled them to continue pursuing or using computer science—both by effectively equipping students with the necessary tools to succeed in future computer science work as well as by elevating their confidence to keep going.

How I Teach

Learning by Doing

Students regularly practice concepts both inside and oustide of the classroom, through programming assignments, effective code design, and error analysis.

Integrating Research-Based Learning

I designed research-based activities to enhance student learning at the undergraduate level and the graduate level, bringing in contemporary NLP research and advanced computing questions into the classroom.

Collaborative Learning

Computing doesn't happen in isolation: programmers regularly code in existing large code-bases, and develop code which others will use. I use collaborative learning as a way to ensure students learn to leverage others' voices and work as a team.
